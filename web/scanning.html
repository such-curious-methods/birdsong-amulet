Scanning for signatures

Capture Bluetooth advertisements and help the Amulet recognize new devices.

Ground rules

  • Capture only devices you own, or ones whose owner said yes. Scans of shops and streets are good for ideas but do not count as evidence.
  • A signature matches a kind of device, never one particular device.
  • Leave Bluetooth addresses and device names out of what you share.

The quick way: a phone app

nRF Connect for Mobile (Android and iOS) lists nearby devices and shows each advertisement's raw bytes. Android shows the most detail.

With an ESP32: the scanning script

ble_scan.py is a small MicroPython script that prints every kind of advertisement an ESP32 hears, with its raw bytes and the decoded fields. Like the Amulet, it only listens. It hides addresses and names: each device gets a short label like dev-88babc that changes every run.

This replaces the Amulet firmware. Use a spare ESP32 if you have one. To turn it back into an Amulet afterwards, use Install or update firmware and choose Fresh install. Your saved call and answer will be reset.
  1. Install Thonny, a beginner-friendly Python editor.
  2. Plug in the ESP32. In Thonny's interpreter settings, choose MicroPython (ESP32) and let Thonny install MicroPython on the board.
  3. Download ble_scan.py, open it in Thonny, and press Run.

The output looks like this:

Scanning for 60 s; press Ctrl-C to stop.
dev-88babc  -60 dBm  random address  connectable
  raw  02 01 06 08 09 xx xx xx xx xx xx xx 02 0a f4 04 16 5a fd 13
  flags 0x06
  name (hidden, 7 bytes)
  tx power -12 dBm
  service data 0xFD5A (Samsung SmartTag): 13

Capturing your own device

Change the settings at the top of the script:

SettingWhat it does
MIN_RSSI = -50Ignore weak signals. Hold your device right next to the board to capture only it.
ONLY_COMPANY_ID = 0x004CShow one maker only; this example is Apple.
DURATION_S = 0Scan until you press Ctrl-C.
SHOW_NAMES = TrueShow device names. Only for your own devices.

On a Linux laptop, bluetoothctl (type scan on) gives a quick view, and btmon shows raw bytes.

What to send

  • The device: make, model, and firmware version if you know it.
  • Its state in each capture: near its owner's phone or away, paired or not.
  • Several captures of the raw bytes in each state.
  • Near misses: similar devices that should not match, such as an iPhone next to an AirTag.
  • Where the format is documented, if anywhere: a spec, an app's source code, a paper.
