Bluetooth basics

How Bluetooth works, and why it says so much about us.

Bluetooth vs Wi-Fi

Both use the same 2.4 GHz radio band, but they do different jobs. Wi-Fi is for networking: a device joins an access point and moves lots of data to and from the internet, using more power. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) is for short, occasional messages between nearby devices, on very little power.

Wi-FiBluetooth Low Energy
Built forInternet access, big files, videoSmall messages: sensors, trackers, watches
PowerHigherVery low; coin cells can last a year
Typical rangeTens of metersRoughly 10 to 50 meters
Talking to strangersJoin a network firstBroadcasts that anyone nearby can hear

Advertising: the part that matters here

BLE devices announce themselves with advertisements: short packets, up to 31 bytes, sent many times a second. Anyone nearby can hear them without pairing or connecting. That is how your phone finds earbuds, how trackers report in, and how the Amulet notices trackers, drones and other Amulets.

It is also why Bluetooth leaks so much. Advertisements can carry a maker's ID, a device type, a name like "Maria's Watch", or an address that stays the same for days.

Classic Bluetooth, used for streaming audio to headphones, and BLE share a name and often a chip, but they are different protocols.
